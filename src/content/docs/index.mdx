## It models the whole instrument, on purpose

Aconite is an analog-modeling polysynth built by **Alkaloid Audio**. Most synths
hand you a set of good parts and leave it to you to make them cohere. Aconite is
built the other way round: it models the *whole* instrument, including the parts
you never see on a panel, so the pieces settle into a single voice instead of
stacking into layers.

Some of that lives in the sound sources. The string model has a full resonating
body behind it that any source can drive. The chip oscillator carries the real
output stage of the console it models, so stacking its channels blends them the way
the hardware does. The rest is the glue further down the chain: console-style bus
saturation, a power supply that sags and blooms under a heavy chord, and a
sympathetic resonance that lets held strings ring with what you play. None of these
are effects bolted on the end. They are the connective tissue that makes a signal
path sound like an instrument.

## The parts are meant to interact

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Many ways to make a tone" icon="seti:audio">
    Three full oscillators plus sub and noise: classic virtual-analog and
    wavetable, a **drawable additive** engine with per-partial analog drift, and
    physical-modeling **String** (plucked, hammered piano, and bowed), **Modal**
    percussion (fourteen tuned instruments), a flexible **Kick**, and a **chip**
    oscillator. Cross-modulated with true through-zero FM and band-limited hard sync.
  </Card>
  <Card title="It rings with what you play" icon="approve-check">
    A built-in bank of tuned string resonators sounds in sympathy with your notes:
    the piano sustain-pedal bloom, a tanpura or sitar drone, a harp's open-string
    shimmer. A per-oscillator send lets **any** patch take on a sympathetic halo,
    with a damper-aware mode that retunes the bank to the notes you are holding.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Analog character everywhere, placed by you" icon="random">
    Not warmth at one stage: analog-modeled oscillators, per-voice and per-partial
    drift, saturable filter poles, a driven voice mixer, console bus glue, and a
    power-supply sag that blooms under heavy chords. Most of those amounts are
    modulation destinations, so you move the character in real time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Filters worth learning" icon="setting">
    Two independent filters, **seven models each** (Ladder, SVF, Bite, Multimode,
    Cascade, Diode, Acid), a continuous mode-morph you can modulate, per-filter
    feedback "bark", in-loop saturation for real analog growl, and resonance that
    self-oscillates into a pure sine you can play as a voice. Series, parallel, or
    split.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Everything can move, and you draw it

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Modulation, drawn by hand" icon="pencil">

  </Card>
  <Card title="Closer to a groovebox" icon="seti:clock">

  <Card title="A full effects rack" icon="puzzle">

    multi-tap, reverb, tape, distortion, bit-crusher, compressor, transient shaper,
    EQ, and stereo imager.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A showpiece you can re-skin" icon="seti:image">
    A dense interface with real-signal visualisers and a full theme editor: edit
    every colour live, build a theme from scratch or from an existing one, and save,
    load, or embed it in a preset.
  </Card>

## Who it's for

Aconite is for people who reach for a synth to **shape** a sound, not just pick one
off a shelf. It is equally happy loading a factory patch and playing, or letting you
build a tone from the ground up. If you want physical instruments that respond
instead of trigger, character controls that interact, and a modulation system deep
enough to make a patch breathe, it rewards every hour you put in.

## Formats

Aconite ships as **VST3**, **AU**, and a **Standalone** app, for **macOS** and
**Windows**.
