## It sounds like hardware because it models hardware

Aconite is an analog-modeling polysynth built by **Alkaloid Audio**. Analog-modeled
filters and oscillators, per-voice character and drift, and a signal path you
reroute rather than a fixed chain. It is equally happy loading a factory patch and
playing, or letting you build a tone from the filter up.

## Everything serves the filters

This is the idea the whole instrument is organized around. Two fully independent
filters, each drawn from a menu of seven models, sit at the center, and the
oscillators, modulation, and effects all exist to feed them and move them.

<Card title="Two filters, front and center" icon="setting">
  Two independent filters, **seven models each** (Ladder, SVF, Bite, Multimode,
  Cascade, Diode, Acid), a continuous mode-morph you can modulate, per-filter
  feedback "bark" for growl and self-oscillation, and a live response graph you
  grab and drag. Series, parallel, or split, this is where a patch gets its
  character.
</Card>

Everything below exists to give those filters something worth shaping.

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Many ways to make a tone" icon="seti:audio">
    Classic virtual-analog and wavetable oscillators alongside physical-modeling
    String, Modal, and Kick models, cross-modulated with **true through-zero FM**
    and band-limited hard sync. Three full oscillators plus sub and noise.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Everything can move" icon="random">
    A per-voice modulation matrix, bus modulation over the effects and master,
    six drawable LFOs, six routable envelopes, plus an arpeggiator and step
    sequencer that double as modulation sources.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A full effects rack" icon="puzzle">
    Twelve effects, freely re-orderable, each insert or send/return: chorus,
    phaser, delay, multi-tap, reverb, tape, distortion, bit-crusher, compressor,
    transient shaper, EQ, and stereo imager.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Who it's for

Aconite is for people who reach for a synth to **sculpt** a sound, not pick one
off a shelf. If loading patch 34 and moving on is all you want, this is not the
instrument for you. If you want filters with nerd knobs worth learning, character
controls that interact, and a modulation system deep enough to make a patch
breathe, it rewards every hour you put in.

## Formats

Aconite ships as **VST3**, **AU**, and a **Standalone** app, for **macOS** and
**Windows**.
