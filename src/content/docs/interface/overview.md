Aconite's window is organized into horizontal **bands**, stacked top to bottom.
Each band owns one stage of the instrument, so the layout reads roughly in signal
order: where sound is made at the top, how it is shaped in the middle, and how it
is performed toward the bottom.

Rather than reproduce the whole tall window here, each chapter shows a close-up of
its own band, so you see the controls at a readable size where they are explained.

## The bands, top to bottom

Reading down the window, you move through the instrument:

- **Header** — the preset browser, A/B compare, theme switcher, MIDI-learn arm,
  the PANIC button, the Options (gear) menu, and the oversampling Quality control.
- **Sources** — the oscillators, sub, and noise, plus the mixer.
- **Filter · Modulation · Effects** — the two filters and their routing, the
  modulation system, and the effects rack.
- **Master** — the output stage: gain, glue, sag, and metering.
- **Scope** — the live visualizations, monitoring the master output.
- **Performance** — voicing and play controls.
- **Arp · Scale · Step sequencer** — the pattern engine.
- **Keyboard** — the on-screen keyboard with the pitch and mod wheels.

Bands can be collapsed to save space, and several sections can be popped out for a
focused view. As you work through this manual, each chapter zooms into one band.

Because Aconite is deeply configurable, it leans hard on consistency: once you
learn how a knob, a right-click menu, or the theming works in one place, it works
the same everywhere. The next chapters cover those shared behaviors before diving
